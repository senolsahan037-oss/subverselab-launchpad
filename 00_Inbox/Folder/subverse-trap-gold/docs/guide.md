## Overview

Trap Gold Vol. 1 is a collection of 100 trap drum MIDI loops. Every file is a standard MIDI pattern, not audio — you drag each loop onto a drum rack or sampler of your own choice inside your DAW, and it plays through whatever kit you load. Nothing here is pre-rendered audio, so the sound is entirely down to the samples you pair it with.

## What's actually in the pack

100 individual .mid files, each an 8-bar drum pattern. Tempos span 130 to 155 BPM across the set, and each filename states its own tempo and style tag so you can find what you need without opening every file. Total download size is small, under 1 MB, because MIDI stores note data, not audio.

## Style categories

The 100 loops are organized across twelve style tags, roughly eight loops in each: Hard Atlanta, Dark Melodic, Rage, Phonk, Modern Producer, Minimal Heavy, Hype Club, Fast Roll, Drill, Cyber, and Bouncy, plus a handful of early Atmospheric-tagged patterns. These are descriptive tags carried over from the filenames, not a guarantee of a specific drum-kit sound — they describe the rhythmic feel of the pattern itself (how the hi-hats roll, how sparse or busy the kick placement is), which comes through regardless of which kit you load underneath it.

## How to use it

1. Download the pack and unzip it.
2. Drag any .mid file directly onto a MIDI or drum track in your DAW.
3. Load your own kick, snare, hi-hat, and 808 samples onto the pads the pattern is written for, or point it at an existing drum rack you already use.
4. Adjust the host tempo to match the BPM in the filename, or use it as a starting tempo and drag it to taste.

Because these are MIDI patterns rather than audio loops, there is no time-stretching or pitch artifact to worry about — changing tempo is just changing your project's BPM.

## What this pack does not include

There is no audio content in this pack. No pre-rendered WAV loops and no included 808 or drum samples — you supply the sounds, the pack supplies the rhythm. There's also no audio preview available for this pack for the same reason: previewing a MIDI file honestly requires a sound source, and this pack intentionally doesn't bundle one.
